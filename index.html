Student Researcher · Singapore

I study how migration reshapes economies — and the families inside them.

Most of the world's attention to migration stops at the border. I'm more interested in what happens at the kitchen table — the household that sends someone away, and the economy quietly built on their absence.

About

I'm a student at UWCSEA Dover in Singapore — a city built, in large part, by people who left somewhere else to be here. That fact sat in the background of my life for years before I started asking what it actually costs, and who pays.

My work circles a single question: what does a country trade away when it exports its labor, and what do the families left behind actually carry? The Philippines became my case study — a nation that has turned the export of its own people into economic policy. I wanted to understand that as both an economist and as someone who could sit across a table from the people inside the statistics.

So I've chased the question with whatever tool fit. A 10,000-word research paper when the answer lived in the data. A documentary when it lived in someone's face. A podcast when it lived in conversation. And direct work with migrant domestic workers in Singapore when it lived in a room I needed to actually be in. Different methods, one investigation.
